Akt phosphorylates SH3BP4 at S246 which leads to 14-3-3 adapter binding. EXPECTED OUTCOMES A successful co-IP experiment should contain a negative control sample with no co-IP signal (back- ground control) and a clearly detectable but not saturated signal for the positive control sample. The co-IP bait signals should show equal pulldown. The supernatant samples for both bait and prey should show equal protein expression levels. Finally, the loading control should indicate equal pro- tein levels for all samples (Figures 3A and 3B). For strong protein-protein interactions the immunoblot signals likely are distinct and perturbations may induce pronounced changes in the binding capacity. This may lead to more than 5-fold differ- ences in protein binding, which may be statistically highly significant. For weaker protein-protein in- teractions, however, the immunoblot signals are more heterogeneous and the effects of perturbations may be less obvious. Two to four-fold differences are typically observed. Given this variability, with 5-7 repeats from three independent experiments, significance levels may end up in the range of 0.01-0.05 (Figure 4D) (Burckhardt et al., 2021). QUANTIFICATION AND STATISTICAL ANALYSIS From the immunoblots semi-quantitative data can be derived by densitometry. The results from mul- tiple experimental repeats should be combined and statistical tests should be used to assess the significance of the observed differences (Pollard et al., 2019) (Figures 4C and 4D). Here we are ll OPEN ACCESS STAR Protocols 2, 100644, September 17, 2021 21 Protocol proposing to outweigh some of the aforementioned limitations of co-IP experiments by increasing the number of repeats to 3-5 or more, if necessary. If the data points follow a normal distribution the Student T-test can be used to address significance levels. If the data doesn’t follow a normal distri- bution, alternative approaches such as the Wilcoxon rank-sum test should be used. LIMITATIONS As highlighted throughout the protocol, co-IP experiments have considerable limitations (Takahashi, 2015) and each individual step along the protocol is prone to artifacts. The protocols that are referenced above provide in-depth analyses of these pitfalls. However, a couple experimental and analytical limi- tations of this co-IP protocol should be addressed in more detail. First, proteins are transiently overex- pressed at non-physiological levels which may prevent accurate protein folding, mis-localize proteins, prevent or induce posttranslational modifications or lead to proteolytic cleavage. When overexpressed, proteins may artificially interact with one another in ways that do not occur at endogenous expression levels. Furthermore, overexpression may colocalize proteins that under physiological conditions would be separated into different cellular compartments. Additionally, small and large peptide tags are intro- duced to facilitate the co-IP and immunodetection. The tags may interfere with protein structure and function. Additionally, the lysis buffer dissociates membranes and de-compartmentalizes proteins and dilutes binding partners. Consequently, transient interactions that depend on membrane binding, high local crowdingby compartmentalization or have high off-ratesmay be difficultto detect.Moreover, additional co-factors may be expressed at low levels and may become limiting. For all the above rea- sons, it is important to validate the findings from co-IP experiments with purified and characterized pro- teins in-vitro and with orthogonal assays under physiological conditions in-vivo. Frequently, co-IP experiments are presented as a series of immunoblots without a quantification of the protein signals. And most often, data from the experimental repeats are not combined for sta- tistical analysis. Here, we are proposing to combine semi-quantitative immunoblot densitometry and statistical assessments to add numbers to the data. Importantly, this protocol can be imple- mented in labs equipped with standard digital gel imaging systems and it does not require special- ized infrastructure. There are, however, multiple limitations to this approach. First, depending on the nature of the protein interaction, the overexpression of bait and prey proteins may favor complex formation to the extent that regulatory mechanisms are overrun. Therefore, the protein overexpres- sion should be adjusted to remain within the dynamic range of the complex formation. Second, the chemiluminescence based protein detection amplifies signals in a non-linear way. This issue is particularly apparent when super-sensitive ECL reagents are used. Fluorescently labeled antibodies that are detected by fluorescence scanners should be considered to obtain a more quantitative readout. Also, the protein detection should be validated to be in a linear range by serial dilution ex- periments (Janes, 2015). Lastly, to obtain a quantitative understanding of protein interactions, sys- tematic measurements with purified proteins and more sophisticated modalities are needed. Exper- iments where the bound and unbound ligand can be measure in a concentration dependent manner allow for the quantification of binding parameters. Such methods include enzyme-linked immuno- sorbent assay (ELISA), Fo ̈ rster resonance energy transfer (FRET) or surface plasmon resonance (SPR) biosensors. Alternatively, isothermal titration calorimetry (ITC)(Velazquez-Campoy et al., 2004) can be used to determine the thermodynamics of protein-protein interactions. In conclusion, the protocol presented here allows for a cost and time effective interrogation of protein complex formation in cell lysates and adds a semi-quantitative measurement and statistical assessment. How- ever, due to the limitations of the protocol, the results should be validated by quantitative experi- mentation with purified proteins and under physiological settings in-vivo.                                                               Guide

                                                              You do not type anything

                                                              Select the words in the Methods text, then press Use highlighted text. The exact words you selected are what gets recorded.

                                                              Typing is what this design removes. Given a sentence like the one below, one person types RIPA, another types the whole parenthesis, a third types the clause. All three read the paper correctly and disagree in the data. Selecting removes that entirely.

                                                              The cell total protein was isolated using RIPA (Applygen Technologies Inc., Beijing, China). Protease inhibitor (CWBIO, Shanghai, China) was added into the RIPA at a ratio of 1:100.

                                                              For lysis buffer there, mark just RIPA, the first time it appears.

                                                              How much to select

                                                              The shortest phrase that states the detail. Not the whole sentence when three words will do, and not the vendor, city or catalogue number unless the question asks for them.

                                                              Every question shows a worked example under What to mark. Follow it rather than your instinct.

                                                              When a paper gives several

                                                              Many papers list a table of antibodies with eight RRIDs, eight catalogue numbers and several vendors. Mark them all. Press Use highlighted text once per value and they stack up in a numbered list. Press Done, next when the list is complete.

                                                              If they sit in a contiguous table or a run-on list, selecting the whole block in one go is fine and much faster. You are marking where the evidence is, not counting.

                                                              The same value repeated does not need marking twice. A paper that uses 1:1000 for six antibodies has one dilution, not six.

                                                              If you stop before you have them all, tick 'There are more of these than I marked'. That box matters more than it looks: without it, someone who stopped at five of eight cannot be told apart from someone who found everything, and the three they skipped would count against the extractor as mistakes it did not make.

                                                              What counts

                                                              Judge only what is written. Not what the authors almost certainly did, and not what is standard practice in the field.

                                                              Only the Methods section is shown, and that is deliberate. If a detail appears solely in a figure legend or a supplementary file, it is not in the text you are given, so the answer is Not stated.

                                                              Unclear is a real answer. It is set aside rather than counted either way, so use it when you are genuinely stuck rather than guessing.

                                                              The shaded sentences

                                                              Opening a question shades the sentences most likely to hold the answer, and Prev and Next step between them.

                                                              They are a search aid and nothing more. A crude keyword rule picks them, it knows nothing about the answer, it sometimes shades irrelevant sentences and sometimes misses the right one. Read around them. When nothing is shaded, read the section normally.

                                                              Every question, in one place

                                                              RRID or antibody identifierAn RRID for an antibody, such as RRID:AB_2160010, or a bare AB_ accession number. A catalogue number on its own is not an RRID.
                                                              Mark: Mark every distinct identifier, one at a time. If they sit in a table or a run-on list, marking the whole block once is fine and faster. In (anti-GAPDH, RRID:AB_2160010) mark RRID:AB_2160010. A paper listing eight antibodies has eight of these; mark them all, or select the block.
                                                              Gel percentageThe acrylamide percentage of the separating gel.
                                                              Mark: Mark the percentage. For a gradient, mark the whole range. In separated on 10% SDS-PAGE mark 10%. In a 4-12% gradient gel mark 4-12%.
                                                              Primary antibody host or typeThe host species or the clonality of a primary antibody.
                                                              Mark: Mark the host and clonality together where they sit together. Mark each distinct combination once; you do not need to mark rabbit eight times. In rabbit polyclonal anti-p53 mark rabbit polyclonal.
                                                              Quantification software, namedA named software package used to quantify bands. The phrase 'densitometry was performed', with no package named, does not count.
                                                              Mark: Mark the package name only. Leave out the version and the supplier. In quantified in ImageJ (v1.53, NIH) mark ImageJ.
                                                              Antibody catalogue numberA vendor catalogue or product number for any antibody, such as ab1234, #9662, or sc-25778. A lot number on its own does not count.
                                                              Mark: Mark every distinct catalogue number, not the vendor or the antibody name. A table of antibodies can be marked as one block. In (Abcam, ab1234) mark ab1234.
                                                              Protein quantification methodHow total protein was measured before loading.
                                                              Mark: Mark the assay name only. In quantified by BCA assay (Pierce) mark BCA.
                                                              Blocking agent concentrationA percentage attached to the blocking step. A blocking agent with no percentage does not count.
                                                              Mark: Mark the percentage only. In blocked in 5% non-fat dry milk mark 5%.
                                                              Lysis bufferThe lysis or extraction buffer, either named or given as a recipe.
                                                              Mark: Mark the words that name the buffer. Leave out the vendor, the city, and anything added to it such as protease inhibitors. In isolated using RIPA (Applygen Technologies Inc., Beijing, China). Protease inhibitor was added into the RIPA mark just RIPA, the first time it appears.
                                                              Antibody dilutionAny antibody working dilution. Primary and secondary both count, and you are not being asked which is which.
                                                              Mark: Mark each distinct dilution once. If the same dilution is used repeatedly, one mark is enough. In incubated at 1:1000 overnight mark 1:1000.
                                                              Reducing or denaturing conditionsAn explicit statement of reducing or denaturing conditions, whether by name or by reagent.
                                                              Mark: Mark the phrase or the reagent that states it. In boiled in Laemmli buffer containing DTT mark DTT, or boiled in Laemmli buffer if no reagent is named.
                                                              Blocking agentWhat the membrane was blocked in.
                                                              Mark: Mark the agent only. Leave the percentage out, it is a separate question. In blocked in 5% non-fat dry milk mark non-fat dry milk.
                                                              Detection chemistryHow bands were detected: chemiluminescence or ECL, fluorescence, colorimetric, or radiographic film.
                                                              Mark: Mark the words naming the detection method. In visualised by enhanced chemiluminescence mark enhanced chemiluminescence.
                                                              Membrane typePVDF or nitrocellulose, or another named membrane chemistry.
                                                              Mark: Mark the membrane name only. In transferred to a 0.45 um PVDF membrane mark PVDF.
                                                              Loading control, namedA named loading control protein. The phrase 'a loading control was used', with no protein named, does not count.
                                                              Mark: Mark each distinct protein name. A paper using GAPDH for one blot and tubulin for another has two. In normalised to GAPDH mark GAPDH.
                                                              Protein loaded per laneA mass of protein loaded per lane. A volume on its own does not count.
                                                              Mark: Mark the mass and its unit. In 30 ug of protein was loaded per lane mark 30 ug.
                                                              Primary antibody vendorThe company that supplied a primary antibody.
                                                              Mark: Mark every distinct company name. Leave out the city and country. A table can be marked as one block. In (Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA) mark Cell Signaling Technology.
                                                              Acquisition softwareSoftware that ran the imager while the blot was being captured. This is separate from the package used afterwards to quantify bands.
                                                              Mark: Mark the software name only. In images captured with Image Lab software mark Image Lab.
                                                              ECL reagent, namedA named ECL or substrate product. The word ECL on its own, with no product name, does not count.
                                                              Mark: Mark the product name. Leave out the manufacturer unless the name alone would not identify it. In SuperSignal West Pico (Thermo) mark SuperSignal West Pico.
                                                              Transfer methodHow protein was moved onto the membrane: wet or tank, semi-dry, dry, or a named transfer system.
                                                              Mark: Mark the words naming the method. In transferred using a semi-dry apparatus mark semi-dry.
                                                              Total protein stainPonceau S, stain-free, REVERT, Coomassie, or another total protein normalisation stain.
                                                              Mark: Mark the stain name only. In confirmed by Ponceau S staining mark Ponceau S.
                                                              Membrane pore sizeA membrane pore size in micrometres.
                                                              Mark: Mark the number and its unit. In a 0.45 um PVDF membrane mark 0.45 um.
                                                              Imager instrumentThe imaging hardware, including X-ray film where film was used.
                                                              Mark: Mark the instrument name only. Leave out the manufacturer. In imaged on a ChemiDoc MP (Bio-Rad) mark ChemiDoc MP.

                                                              Ask about a rule

                                                              This answers questions about what a rule means, nothing else. It is never given the paper, it cannot see what you are reading, and it will refuse if you paste text and ask what the answer is. Deciding what a paper says is the whole job being measured here, so it has to stay yours.
