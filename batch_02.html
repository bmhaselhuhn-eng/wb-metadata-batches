In-vivo e-cig vaping Pregnant CD1 mice were exposed (via direct inhalation) to e-cig vapor containing 2.4% nicotine (BluTM, 24 mg/mL nicotine) mixed with oxygenated air or oxygenated air alone, six times/day; 2 cartridges/day from gestational day 5 (E5) until delivery. A modified CORESTA (Cooperation Centre for Scientific Research Relative to Tobacco) standard smoking protocol adapted to study e-cig exposure (27.5 mL puff depth volume, 3 s puff duration, 2 puffs per 60 s, 32 puffs/session) was followed in the laboratory. E-cig vapor was generated using a Single Cigarette Smoking Machines (SCSM, CH Technologies Inc.) following a previously published method used by our laboratory (Kaisar et al., 2017; Sifat et al., 2018; Sifat et al., 2020). This method was developed to mimic the smoking behavior of a human chronic/heavy smoker/vaper and yields plasma levels of cotinine (111 ng/mL) which is in the range of blood cotinine level (30-250 ng/mL) found in other preclinical rodent models of chronic e-cig exposure (McGrath-Morrow et al., 2015; Shi et al., 2019).                                                               Guide

                                                              You do not type anything

                                                              Select the words in the Methods text, then press Use highlighted text. The exact words you selected are what gets recorded.

                                                              Typing is what this design removes. Given a sentence like the one below, one person types RIPA, another types the whole parenthesis, a third types the clause. All three read the paper correctly and disagree in the data. Selecting removes that entirely.

                                                              The cell total protein was isolated using RIPA (Applygen Technologies Inc., Beijing, China). Protease inhibitor (CWBIO, Shanghai, China) was added into the RIPA at a ratio of 1:100.

                                                              For lysis buffer there, mark just RIPA, the first time it appears.

                                                              How much to select

                                                              The shortest phrase that states the detail. Not the whole sentence when three words will do, and not the vendor, city or catalogue number unless the question asks for them.

                                                              Every question shows a worked example under What to mark. Follow it rather than your instinct.

                                                              When a paper gives several

                                                              Many papers list a table of antibodies with eight RRIDs, eight catalogue numbers and several vendors. Mark them all. Press Use highlighted text once per value and they stack up in a numbered list. Press Done, next when the list is complete.

                                                              If they sit in a contiguous table or a run-on list, selecting the whole block in one go is fine and much faster. You are marking where the evidence is, not counting.

                                                              The same value repeated does not need marking twice. A paper that uses 1:1000 for six antibodies has one dilution, not six.

                                                              If you stop before you have them all, tick 'There are more of these than I marked'. That box matters more than it looks: without it, someone who stopped at five of eight cannot be told apart from someone who found everything, and the three they skipped would count against the extractor as mistakes it did not make.

                                                              What counts

                                                              Judge only what is written. Not what the authors almost certainly did, and not what is standard practice in the field.

                                                              Only the Methods section is shown, and that is deliberate. If a detail appears solely in a figure legend or a supplementary file, it is not in the text you are given, so the answer is Not stated.

                                                              Unclear is a real answer. It is set aside rather than counted either way, so use it when you are genuinely stuck rather than guessing.

                                                              The shaded sentences

                                                              Opening a question shades the sentences most likely to hold the answer, and Prev and Next step between them.

                                                              They are a search aid and nothing more. A crude keyword rule picks them, it knows nothing about the answer, it sometimes shades irrelevant sentences and sometimes misses the right one. Read around them. When nothing is shaded, read the section normally.
